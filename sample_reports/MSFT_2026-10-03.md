## Investment thesis

### Bull case
- A $678B backlog, up 84%, gives multi-year revenue visibility. Azure is growing 40%+ on a base above $100B.
- Operating margin expanded to 46.8% even with heavy AI investment. Copilot and agent monetization on the roughly 17%-growing M365 base is not yet fully reflected in the numbers.
- FY27 guidance of double-digit growth, opex growth below revenue growth, and flat share count supports about 20%+ EPS growth. At 21.9x forward that is a reasonable multiple.
- Balance sheet and cash flow are strong enough to fund both capex and $48.7B a year of shareholder returns.

### Bear case
- FCF has fallen from $74.1B (FY24) to $67.0B (FY26) while revenue grew 35%. Capex at 35% of revenue is a bet that demand persists. If the AI cycle slows, depreciation (already up from $21B to $38.5B in two years) will compress margins.
- Part of the backlog and Azure growth is tied to OpenAI and a few AI labs. That concentration risk is visible in the backlog (my judgement).
- FY26 EPS growth of 32% is partly non-operating. On a TTM P/E of 28.6x, the stock is not cheap on underlying earnings.
- The stock rose 33% in 3 months and sits 7% below its 52-week high of $553.72. It is volatile (32.7% annualized) and a failed capex or Azure print could revisit the 200-day average of $431.
- Useful-life extension and lease reclassification flatter near-term operating income and capex optics. Skeptics will discount this.

3. **OpenAI dependence and relationship:** changes to the partnership terms, revenue share, or OpenAI's finances could affect both earnings (non-operating items) and Azure demand.
4. **Competition:** Alphabet and Amazon are growing faster (24.2% and 19.6% versus MSFT's 17.7% per Yahoo), and AI-native tools threaten the seat-based software model.
5. **Consumer weakness:** Xbox content fell 5% and Windows OEM is flat to down.
6. **Regulation, trade, and FX:** antitrust scrutiny, tariffs, and currency effects, which the 10-K lists as risks.
