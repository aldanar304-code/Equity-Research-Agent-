## 1. Recommendation and thesis
Apple is operating very well, with its strongest June quarter ever. At 34.8x forward earnings, though, the stock is priced for that performance to continue, and none of my DCF scenarios reaches the current price. I rate it Sell with medium conviction. The valuation is the main reason, and the stock's 30.3% one-year gain and strong momentum are the main reason I'm not more confident.

1. **Earnings quality is flattered by one-offs.** June-quarter gross margin of 50.1% included about 2 points from tariff refunds, and EPS included $0.11 from them (8-K-EX-2026-07). Guidance for the September quarter is 47-48% gross margin (Q3 call summary, see sources). Test: a reported September-quarter margin at or below 48% would confirm that the 50% level was not a new baseline.
2. **Growth is decelerating while costs rise.** Revenue growth was 16% in the June quarter and is guided to 9-11% for September. Apple cited supply constraints on advanced chip nodes, higher memory costs and a 2.5-point FX headwind. R&D rose 32% year over year to $11.7B in the June quarter (8-K-EX-2026-07). Test: FY27 revenue growth below the 10.5% consensus.
3. **FCF has not grown while the multiple is high.** TTM free cash flow is $107.7B, versus $108.8B in FY24 (financial history, company profile). The stock trades at 38.3x trailing and 34.8x forward earnings. Test: if FY27 EPS comes in near my $9.40 estimate and FY28 near $10.20, a 28.5x multiple implies about $290 (28.5 × $10.20 = $290.7).
4. **A CEO transition and two legal overhangs add risk.** Tim Cook transitions to Executive Chair and John Ternus becomes CEO, effective Sept 1, 2026 (8-K-2026-04-2). The Google search-licensing remedies and the App Store injunction remain live issues (10-Q-2026-06).

## 3. Industry and competitive position
Apple competes in premium smartphones, PCs and tablets, and in digital services. In my view its moat comes from the installed base (a record across all major categories and regions per the Q3 release), ecosystem lock-in, brand and scale. Weaknesses are dependence on third-party components and manufacturing (listed in the 8-K risk language), tariff and trade exposure, and regulators targeting the App Store and search-licensing economics.

## 8. Key risks and mitigants (ranked)
1. **Multiple compression.** At 34.8x forward earnings, any growth miss could re-rate the stock. This is also the main risk to my Sell call, since momentum could persist. Signal: the September-quarter result and the holiday-quarter outlook.
2. **Margin pressure from memory and chip costs.** Apple states that gross margins will be "subject to volatility and downward pressure" (10-Q-2026-03). Memory costs were already a drag in the June quarter (10-Q-2026-06). According to a call summary (VectorShift), Apple has raised iPad and Mac prices, which is a partial mitigant.
3. **Google search-licensing remedies.** The D.C. court ordered remedies on Sept 2, 2025 and both sides have appealed. A reversal could impose DOJ-proposed remedies, such as prohibiting Google from offering Apple commercial terms for search distribution (10-Q-2026-06). I did not verify the exact current terms or the size of the revenue at stake.
4. **App Store regulation and litigation.** The April 30, 2025 injunction bars commissions on purchases outside apps and is on appeal to the Ninth Circuit. The DOJ monopolization suit is pending. EU Digital Markets Act fines can reach up to 10% of annual worldwide net sales (10-Q-2025-12).
5. **Supply constraints and tariffs.** Advanced-node supply is limited (Q3 call summary), and Greater China is 17.8% of nine-month sales. Tariff refunds boosted results, and a policy reversal could cost margin.
6. **CEO transition.** Ternus has an engineering background and no prior CEO record, while Cook stays as Executive Chair. The board is stable under Lead Independent Director Levinson.
7. **AI positioning.** The Siri AI launch is new and unproven. R&D spending is rising fast (+32% in the June quarter), and returns on it are unknown.
