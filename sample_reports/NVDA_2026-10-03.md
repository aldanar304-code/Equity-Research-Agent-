## Business overview
NVIDIA designs accelerated-computing platforms: GPUs, CPUs, networking, systems and software (CUDA). It reports two segments. **Compute & Networking** ($88.3B in Q2, 92% of revenue) covers data-center AI systems such as Blackwell, Blackwell Ultra and the new Vera Rubin. **Graphics** ($7.9B) covers gaming GPUs and RTX workstations.

By market, Data Center is 93% of revenue. Within it, Hyperscale is $48.7B, up 102% year over year, and AI Clouds, Industrial & Enterprise is $40.3B, up 138%, driven by AI-native companies, enterprises and sovereign buyers. Edge Computing is $7.2B, up 27%.

Its competitive moat is the full stack. Hardware, interconnect and the CUDA software ecosystem are sold as complete rack-scale systems, which competitors so far match only in parts.

## Investment thesis
### Bull case
Demand still exceeds supply. Management says it faces supply constraints even as commitments more than doubled in one quarter, from $119B to $279B. The Vera Rubin ramp extends NVIDIA's product lead. Management expects Rubin to be about 20% of Q3 Data Center revenue, and NVIDIA puts the revenue opportunity at roughly $40B per gigawatt of capacity. AI agents consume far more compute than chat, so demand for inference is broadening beyond model training. At under 15x forward earnings, even modest growth beyond FY28 supports upside.

### Bear case
NVIDIA is increasingly financing its own demand. It guarantees leases for customers, commits to buy cloud capacity, holds $99B of equity stakes in its ecosystem and is helping set up financing platforms targeting more than $500B. If AI-cloud tenants struggle to fund their build-outs, NVIDIA bears both lost revenue and credit losses. Gross margin is guided down to 71–72%. Big customers such as hyperscalers and OpenAI are also building their own chips. On this view, the low multiple is right because current earnings sit near a cyclical peak.

## Key risks
1. **Customer concentration.** One direct customer was 16% of Q2 revenue, and three customers made up 44% of first-half revenue.
2. **Supply and financing commitments.** These include $279B of supply obligations, lease guarantees for the OpenAI/SB Energy campus capped at $105B, and $36B of cloud-service commitments. All of them depend on how customers perform.
3. **Margin pressure.** Higher memory prices and the Rubin transition lead to guidance of a 71–72% gross-margin trough in Q4 FY27.
4. **China and export controls.** H200 sales to China are restricted by both governments, licensed shipments face a 25% U.S. tariff, and China is under 1% of Data Center revenue.
5. **Infrastructure bottlenecks.** Customers' access to land, power and capital limits how fast systems can be deployed.
6. **Volatility.** The stock's beta is 2.2, its one-year maximum drawdown was -20%, and a two-month selloff in 2026 erased more than $1T of market value before the recovery.

## Catalysts
